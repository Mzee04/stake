A commitment contract on Tari Ootle

Lock some TARI against a private goal.

A person you already trust decides if you kept it. Only a hash of the goal goes on the chain — so a debt, a health goal, or a habit never sits on a public ledger, or on someone else’s server.

Try it without a wallet How it works

The simulation is the full product: create a stake, send a referee a link, rule, and see where the money goes. No wallet, no network. The on-chain path is ready once a template is published.

The referee can lie.

Stake is only as honest as the person you name, the same way stickK is. The chain cannot see whether you hit your goal. It only holds the money and follows the referee’s call — or, if they never answer, the default you picked at the start.

How Stake works

A short, honest account of the product, the chain, and the limits.

Why Tari

People will put money on a goal. They will not always put the goal itself on a public chain, or in a company’s database. A weight target, a debt, a promise to quit something — that is private. Stake stores only sha256("stake/goal/v1" || goal text || 32-byte salt). Anyone with the text and the salt can check that they match the hash. Nobody else can read the goal from the chain.

The amount is public. It sits in a component vault, the same way many Ootle apps hold funds. The privacy claim is the goal, not the number.

The rules, on-chain

  1. Open. You lock a positive amount, a goal hash, a referee’s public key, your account (for a refund), a sink account, a deadline epoch, a grace period, and a timeout default. The deadline must be in the future. You are the transaction signer — the staker.
  2. Rule, success. Only the referee, and only before the timeout epoch (deadline + grace). The funds are deposited back to your account.
  3. Rule, fail. Only the referee, same window. The funds are deposited to the sink.
  4. Timeout. Anyone, at or after the timeout epoch, if nobody has ruled. The default you chose at the start runs: refund, or pay the sink.

A second ruling is rejected. A timeout before the window closes is rejected. A call from anyone but the referee is rejected. The template checks all of this; the engine tests cover each path.

What this will not do

  • The referee can lie. There is no automatic evidence (no Strava, no photo proof that the chain will accept). Pick someone you trust, and who will actually answer.
  • If you name yourself as referee, this is just a delayed refund button. That’s allowed, and not much of a commitment.
  • The sink account has to exist by the time funds are sent to it. If a deposit fails, the whole transaction fails and can be retried once the account is created — the stake stays open.
  • The share link that includes your goal is secret only as long as you keep it. Treat it like the goal itself.
